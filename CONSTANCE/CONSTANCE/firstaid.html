First Aid Basics

Essential emergency response knowledge. Note: Always call emergency services for serious situations.

Burns

Cool the burn under cool (not cold) running water for 10–20 minutes. Do NOT apply butter, toothpaste, oil, or ice. Cover loosely with a clean, dry, non-stick cloth. Seek medical help immediately for large, deep, or facial burns.

Choking

Encourage the person to cough forcefully. If they cannot cough, give up to 5 firm back blows between the shoulder blades. If still blocked, perform up to 5 abdominal thrusts (Heimlich maneuver). Call emergency services immediately if the blockage persists.

Severe Bleeding

Apply firm, direct pressure to the wound with a clean cloth or bandage. Elevate the injured area above the heart if possible. Do not remove the cloth if it soaks through; add more layers on top. Seek immediate medical help for deep, spurting, or uncontrolled bleeding.

Fainting

Lay the person flat on their back and gently raise their legs. Loosen any tight clothing around the neck or waist. Ensure the area has fresh, circulating air. If they do not regain consciousness within 1 minute, call for emergency help.

Suspected Stroke

Remember FAST: Face drooping, Arm weakness, Speech difficulty, Time to call. Call emergency services immediately and note the exact time symptoms started. Keep the person calm, still, and do not give them anything to eat or drink.

Suspected Heart Attack

Call emergency services immediately. Do not drive the person yourself. Have the person sit down, rest, and stay calm. Loosen tight clothing. If prescribed, assist them with their nitroglycerin. If they become unresponsive and stop breathing, begin CPR if you are trained.
